# UKRI Future Leaders Fellowships (FLF) Guidelines

> Based on **Round 11 Guidance for Academic-hosted Applicants** (January 2025, v1.1). Always cross-check the current round's Funding Finder page and the UKRI Standard Grant Terms and Conditions before submitting — word counts, caps, and funding rules change between rounds. Non-academic-hosted applicants (including Catapults) apply via the **Innovation Funding Service (IFS)**, not the Funding Service, and follow separate guidance.

## Scheme Overview

**Funder**: UK Research and Innovation (UKRI) — the umbrella body for the UK's seven research councils plus Innovate UK and Research England.

**Purpose**: Develop and retain the most talented early-career researchers and innovators, and attract new talent to the UK. The scheme funds the **person and their leadership development** as much as the project — this is the defining feature versus a standard research grant.

**Key characteristics**:
- **4(+3) model**: Four years of funding, extendable by up to three more (applied for at a year-4 review). Academic-hosted fellows are expected to take the full seven years. Only the **first four years** are costed and peer-reviewed at application stage.
- **Funding envelope**: Typically up to ~£1.5M. Applications **over £1.4M** must notify UKRI in advance of submission.
- **Cross-disciplinary and cross-sector**: Open to research *and* innovation; supports intersectoral mobility (secondments, placements, rotational appointments).
- **Host commitment required**: The host organisation must commit to a tapered salary contribution from year 3 (Full Economic Costing model) and to providing a **route to an open-ended UK-based position** during or after the fellowship.
- **Host organisation caps**: The number of applications per host is capped (see the round's Funding Finder). Hosts run an internal prioritisation/selection process — most candidates must first win an **internal competition** before they can submit.
- **Renewable**: Fellows may apply to renew in the final year.

**Innovation** is explicitly in scope, defined as "the practical translation of disruptive ideas into novel, relevant and valued products, services, processes, systems or business models … creating economic and/or social value from ideas."

## Eligibility

- Follows **UKRI standard eligibility**. UKRI does **not** advise individuals on eligibility — the decision rests with applicant and host.
- **No fixed career-stage or years-since-PhD limit.** A PhD is not mandatory; equivalent experience counts. Year of PhD completion is collected for monitoring only and is **not** used in assessment. Career stage is judged holistically (experience "appropriate to career stage" recurs throughout the criteria).
- **Part-time**: Allowed down to 0.5 FTE to combine with personal responsibilities (not to combine with another job). Value is pro-rata; duration extends correspondingly.
- **Job-share**: Encouraged and explicitly supported (Annex B).
- **Career breaks / flexible working**: Taken into account by the panel when assessing track record (Annex C). Applicants disclose dates only; no reason required.
- **Overseas applicants**: Eligible; must meet UK visa requirements. Successful applicants qualify for the fast-track **Global Talent Visa**. Visa and Immigration Health Surcharge costs are eligible for the applicant and named DI staff (≥50% time), but not family members.

## Application Structure (UKRI Funding Service)

Applications are submitted through the **UKRI Funding Service (TFS)**. The form is a series of discrete questions, each with its own word count or page limit. The narrative core:

| Section | Limit | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Application title & summary** | Title ≤240 chars; Summary ≤550 words | Summary is published publicly — plain English, no confidential data |
| **Core team** | Structured | Roles: fellow, research & innovation associate, visiting researcher, specialist, technician, professional enabling staff. Only **one** fellow (unless job-share) |
| **Vision and Approach** | **5 sides A4** (PDF upload) | The main case for support — see below |
| **Applicant capability to deliver** | **1,650 words** (1,150 R4RI + 500 Additions) | Narrative R4RI format, **not** a CV |
| **Career development** | **1,000 words** | FLF-distinctive — see below |
| **Host organisation support** | **1,000 words** | Written from the host's point of view |
| **Ethics and responsible research & innovation (RRI)** | **500 words** | Plus optional ethics sub-questions (700 words each) |
| **Resources and cost justification** | Structured + justification | See Resources below |
| **Data management plan** | Structured | Must comply with UKRI data-sharing policy; cost it in |

Conditional sections (complete only if relevant): Resubmissions, Instrument development, Project partners (+ letters of support), Trusted Research and Innovation, Clinical Fellowships, Research involving animals / animals overseas / human participants / human tissue, Facilities, Clinical research using NHS resources (SoECAT).

## Vision and Approach (5 sides A4)

The heart of the application. Single PDF, **≤5 sides A4**, single-spaced, **11-point Arial** (or equivalent sans-serif), margins ≥2cm. May include images, graphs, tables. **Mandatory**: a legible diagrammatic workplan (PERT or Gantt chart) on **one full page** covering the whole project with emphasis on the first four years, milestones and timelines clearly shown.

> File naming: `APPXXXX Vision and Approach` (APPXXXX = the Funding Service application number). Single PDF ≤8MB. **Non-compliant attachments cause the application to be rejected** — formatting is a hard gate, not a guideline.

For a 4(+3) application, make the **long-term vision** clear and explain why it matters, but only give detailed plans/costings for the first four years.

### Vision — address how the work:
- is of **excellent quality and importance** within or beyond the field
- has potential to **advance understanding** or generate new knowledge/thinking/discovery
- is **timely**, given current trends, context, and needs
- impacts world-leading research, society, the economy, or the environment
- identifies potential **direct or indirect benefits** and **who the beneficiaries** are

### Approach — show how the work is designed so that it:
- is **effective and appropriate** to achieve the objectives
- is **feasible**, and comprehensively identifies **risks to delivery** and mitigations
- uses a **clear, transparent methodology** (if applicable)
- summarises previous work and how you will **build on** it (if applicable)
- will **maximise translation** of outputs into outcomes and impacts
- demonstrates **access** to the services, facilities, infrastructure, or equipment needed

**Writing strategy**: This maps closely to a research-council "case for support" but is half the length of many — ruthless prioritisation is essential. Lead with the vision's ambition (panels reward boldness — see Person Specification), then de-risk it with a credible, feasibility-anchored approach. The Gantt page is not optional padding; reviewers use it to judge deliverability.

## Applicant capability to deliver (1,650 words — R4RI)

Evidence of your ability to deliver, **appropriate to career stage**. Use the **Résumé for Research and Innovation (R4RI)** narrative format — **not** a traditional CV. Split: **1,150 words** for R4RI modules (including references) + up to **500 words** "Additions".

Provide evidence of how you have:
- the **relevant experience** (appropriate to career stage) to make best use of this opportunity to develop your career
- the right **balance of skills and aptitude** to deliver the work
- contributed to developing a **positive research environment** and wider community
- the appropriate **team-working or leadership** skills (appropriate to career stage)

**Additions** (optional, ≤500 words): context for the rest of the R4RI — e.g. career breaks, unusual circumstances. **Not** for extra skills/outputs.

> R4RI is built around four modules: (1) contributions to **knowledge/ideas**; (2) contributions to **development of others**; (3) contributions to the **research/innovation community**; (4) contributions to **broader society**. Write in narrative prose. UKRI's "Peer Exchange Platform — Support for R4RI CVs" is the reference.

## Career development (1,000 words) — FLF-distinctive

**This section has no real equivalent in US grants** and is weighted heavily. Focus on *why this fellowship is the right way to develop your career* and *how you will benefit others*. Identify:
- **career development goals** appropriate to this funding opportunity
- how the fellowship gives a **feasible, appropriate trajectory** for your personal development and goals (appropriate to career stage and field)
- how you will **instigate positive change in the wider community** — e.g. EDI, advocacy/advisory roles, stakeholder engagement, peer review, policy influence, public engagement, outreach
- how you will ensure **those you manage** (students, RAs) have a positive experience and support to progress their own careers (cite the Concordat / UKRI Research Integrity framework)
- how the work gives you a trajectory to acquire **additional skills** — research, leadership, communication, management
- proposed **mentoring arrangements** and why they suit you

## Host organisation support (1,000 words)

Written **from the host's perspective**. Must include:
- evidence of how the host will support you, tailored to your development and the fellowship's vision/approach
- **who you engaged with** in the host (name and role)
- how the **research environment** contributes to success (suitability + strategic relevance)
- how the host will **protect your time commitment**
- the **development/training** package, tailored to your aims
- the **financial/practical support** (services, facilities, infrastructure, equipment) and how it strengthens the application
- details of any work at another UK/overseas host (if applicable)

Requires sign-off from a **Pro Vice-Chancellor, Head of Department, or Senior Representative**, including: commitment to the applicant's **salary** and to an **open-ended UK-based independent position** during/after the fellowship; and commitment to **20% of any equipment costs** requested.

## Ethics and responsible research and innovation (RRI) (500 words)

Identify and evaluate the relevant ethical/RRI considerations and how you will manage them. Optional sub-questions (700 words each, only if relevant): genetic/biological risk; vertebrate animals (ASPA template); animals overseas (statement + species templates, NC3Rs review); human participants; human tissue/biological samples.

## Resources and cost justification

Costs follow the **Full Economic Cost (fEC)** model — most costs paid at **80% fEC**; some exceptions at 100%.

- **Directly Incurred**: staff (only time on the project), travel & subsistence, equipment, other (consumables etc.)
- **Directly Allocated**: investigator/estates/indirect — ring-fenced once awarded
- **Equipment**: items >£25,000 (incl. VAT) funded at 80% fEC (except instrument development); host commits to **20%** of equipment costs
- **Exceptions at 100% fEC**: overseas costs; externally commissioned surveys (not run by the applicant)
- **Overseas**: extended periods abroad can claim 100% fEC of overseas staff salaries and direct research costs; >6 months in one period unlocks relocation/living-allowance costs (overseas living allowance generally ≤11% of salary)
- **Ineligible costs**: publication costs, PhD student costs, standard laptops for staff on continuing contracts, contingency costs (e.g. visas for unnamed staff), general care costs, alcohol in subsistence

Data management plan must be included and **costed**.

## Assessment Criteria (Annex A — Person Specification)

The panel and reviewers assess against the person specification. FLF applicants should:
- demonstrate **broad knowledge** of the area and offer a **compelling vision** for the excellence and importance of the work
- take advantage of the **long-term, flexible** support; justify how the proposal has **wider influence** in the field
- have **original, ambitious plans/ideas** that **do not significantly overlap** with their collaborations or **former supervisors** (evidence of **independence** is critical)
- demonstrate suitability of the **environment(s)** for the work and its impact
- give an approach to **maximising impact and influence** (may involve co-production with business, public sector, civil society, community)
- be capable of **leading and developing a team** or taking a leading role in their field; show ability to **identify and maximise potential in others**
- have secured host backing with a commitment to an **open-ended position** at the end
- demonstrate ability to choose and develop **collaborations and networks** (national, international, cross-disciplinary)
- show how outcomes will be **communicated and used** within and beyond their community

**Experience and potential**: necessary skills/knowledge/experience to deliver; a track record of **challenging, original, productive** outputs that stand out; flexibility to embrace new directions.

**Personal development**: identified opportunities for development as **impactful research/innovation leaders** (e.g. work in other environments, international links, new skills like policy impact or commercialisation); identified **career development support** (mentoring, training) — *a clear programme of skills development is an essential component of this fellowship*; strong communication/interpersonal skills developed through engagement with different audiences.

## What makes the FLF distinctive (vs NIH/NSF/DARPA)

- **The fellow is the unit of assessment, not just the project.** Two of the major narrative sections (Career development; Applicant capability) are about *you* and your leadership trajectory. A brilliant project with a weak development case will not win.
- **Leadership and independence are explicitly scored.** Reviewers actively check that ideas don't overlap with former supervisors. Frame yourself as an emerging independent leader.
- **Host commitment is contractual**, not a courtesy letter — salary tapering, an open-ended position, and 20% equipment co-funding must be signed off by a senior representative.
- **Narrative CV (R4RI), not a publication list.** Resist the urge to list papers; tell the story of your contributions across four modules.
- **Strict formatting is a rejection gate** (5 pages, 11pt Arial, 2cm margins, mandatory 1-page Gantt, ≤8MB, specific filename).
- **Internal competition first.** Most hosts cap and pre-select; budget time for the institutional process well ahead of the UKRI deadline.
- **4(+3) framing.** Sell a seven-year vision but cost and de-risk only the first four.

## Drafting Checklist

- [ ] Cleared the host's **internal selection** process and have a host reference engaged
- [ ] Vision and Approach ≤5 pages, 11pt Arial, ≥2cm margins, with a **full-page Gantt/PERT** chart
- [ ] PDF named `APPXXXX Vision and Approach`, ≤8MB, no sensitive personal data
- [ ] Vision sells **ambition + timeliness + beneficiaries**; Approach covers **feasibility + risks + methodology**
- [ ] Applicant capability written as **R4RI narrative** (1,150 + 500 words), not a CV
- [ ] **Independence** from former supervisors/collaborators made explicit
- [ ] Career development (1,000 words) covers goals, trajectory, wider-community change, mentoring, developing others
- [ ] Host support (1,000 words) includes named contacts, protected time, training package, and **senior sign-off** on salary + open-ended position + 20% equipment
- [ ] Ethics/RRI (500 words) plus any triggered sub-questions completed
- [ ] Costs justified at correct fEC %; data management plan included and costed
- [ ] If >£1.4M, **notified UKRI in advance**
- [ ] Cross-checked the **current round's Funding Finder** for changed limits/caps/dates
